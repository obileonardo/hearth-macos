# Accessibility and appearance

Hearth has three appearances, chosen in **Settings → Appearance**: **System** (follows your Mac),
**Light** and **Dark**. The choice applies to the menu bar panel and to every window (camera,
3D map, familiar faces) and changes them straight away.

## How colour works

Every colour comes from a token in `Theme` (`Sources/Hearth/Views/Theme.swift`) that resolves for
the appearance it's drawn in (`Color.adaptive` in `Appearance.swift`):

- **Panel and text:** `base`, `ink`, `muted`, `faint`, `hairline`.
- **Overlays:** fills, chips and quiet lines are `Theme.fg` at a low opacity. `Theme.fg` is white
  in the dark and near-black in the light, so each nested layer sits a little further from the
  background in both.
- **Cards:** on paper they are white, with a soft shadow.
- **Main buttons** (`cta`, `onCTA`): a white pill with black text in the dark, an ink pill with
  white text in the light.
- **Brand blue** (`brand`, `brandEnd`; `sand` is a compatibility alias): richer blue on black,
  with deeper blue on paper. Text and both gradient endpoints exceed 4.5:1 on the stated panel
  and card backgrounds. **Home energy** (`homeEnergy`, `homeEnergyEnd`) uses green for home
  consumption, home illustrations, and demo-home surfaces. Solar, grid, battery, warning and
  AC heat colors retain their separate meanings.
- **Solid blue marks** (`brandFill`, `onBrand`): deeper blue with white text and symbols, including placed-device pins and blue demo badges; both appearances and Increase Contrast fills exceed 4.5:1.
- **Other accents** (`ok`, `live`, the air colours, and colours made with `Color.accent`) resolve
  for their appearance. Generated accents deepen their light variant to at least 4.5:1 on paper.
- **Camera tiles, the live view and the camera window** are screens. They stay dark in either
  appearance, with scrims behind text to support legibility over changing pictures.

## Contrast (WCAG 2.2 AA)

The tables describe token calculations on the stated backgrounds. Live glass, photos, opacity changes and every rendered control still need visual/accessibility review; these calculations do not certify the whole application.

Body and secondary text need 4.5:1. Large text needs 3:1, as do meaningful non-text parts.
The ratios below are computed by `scripts/contrast.py`, which mirrors the tokens; run it again after
changing one. The script also checks text on the blue/green fills and 21 interpolated gradient
stops, including their Increase Contrast variants, and exits with failure below 4.5:1. This
arithmetic does not certify translucent glass, images or every live control.
Use `python3 scripts/contrast.py --verify-source` to also check that the mirrored blue/green
normal and Increase Contrast values still match `Theme.swift` exactly.

- "Panel" is the background.
- "Card" is a card on it: in the dark, the card's translucent layers over the panel; in the light,
  white.

Ordinary hairlines are decorative. Cards are already set apart by their fill, and in the light by
their shadow, so the hairline isn't what identifies them. With Increase Contrast on, hairlines
strengthen to 3:1 or more anyway.

## The system's accessibility settings

| macOS setting | What Hearth does |
|---|---|
| Increase Contrast | Secondary and faint text, hairlines and field edges get stronger (the `darkHC` and `lightHC` values of each token) |
| Reduce Transparency | The glass dock, the settings button and the pinned title bar become solid |
| Reduce Motion | The dock's lens moves without stretching or bouncing. The AC's airflow and the energy particles move at a third of the pace. The charging shimmer is left out. The fan turns slowly. |

Hearth provides VoiceOver labels for icon controls and custom text field placeholders. The newly added compact widgets and map captions use smaller text in some places; readable sizing and keyboard/VoiceOver coverage should be reviewed across the full interface.

## Selected states

A selected segment (Appearance, air conditioner modes, alarm modes, filters) is never shown by colour alone. In light mode the white key sits in a tray darker than the card (tray `#E7E7EB` on a white card), carries a fine edge and a two-layer shadow, and its label switches to ink and semibold while the others stay muted (6.8:1). In dark mode the key is a lit white key on glass. The selected option is also exposed to VoiceOver with the Selected trait.

## Privacy blur

The eye in the Security header (and in the camera window) blurs every camera picture and live feed for screen sharing, leaving labels and controls sharp. It doesn't stop the cameras. With Reduce Motion on, the blur switches without animating.

## Floating navigation

Scrolling pages continue behind the floating navigation capsule without a full-width fade or blur band. Extra trailing scroll padding lets the last control move fully above the dock. Fixed climate controls and the demo exit stay above it. Only the capsule has a glass surface; its transparent surrounding space allows scrolling. Reduce Transparency retains the existing solid dock fallback. The map container groups its controls without replacing their individual labels; Open full map has a separate label and hint.

Navigation glass opacity is adjustable in Settings → Navigation. It changes only the background surface; foreground labels and icons keep full opacity. Reduce Transparency or Increase Contrast forces a solid surface and disables the slider without discarding its saved value. The scrolled heading uses its own capsule instead of a full-width blur. See [navigation](navigation.md).
